## Corrections applied to the first draft

1. **Card-controllability filter.**  Kept, with provenance.  `[LIT-READ]`
   Wang, Wen, Guo, arXiv:2408.02488, Section 3, Theorem 4, attributed to
   Y. Hong, *An eigenvector condition for reconstructibility*, J. Combin.
   Theory Ser. B 32 (1982) 245-256, building on Godsil and McKay, *Spectral
   conditions for the reconstructibility of a graph*, JCTB 30 (1981) 285-289:
   "If there exists a vertex-deleted subgraph `G - u_i` that is controllable,
   then `G` is reconstructible."  Wang, Wen, Guo extend it (Theorem 6) to
   almost controllable cards with a nontrivial automorphism, or asymmetric
   almost controllable cards whose Householder reflection does not carry the
   neighbourhood indicator to another 0-1 vector.  Consequence: every card of
   a counterexample has at most `n-3` main eigenvalues... precisely, every
   card has a non-main eigenvalue.  Generalized cospectrality of a
   counterexample pair follows from Tutte (characteristic polynomial
   reconstructible) applied to `G` and to its complement; `[LIT-READ]` this
   is stated in the same paper's proof of Theorem 6 with citations to Tutte
   1979 and Hagos 2000.  Wang's determinant criterion `[LIT-ABSTRACT]`
   arXiv:2110.09404 and arXiv:1309.6090.
2. **Lazy non-isomorphism.**  The SAT encoding (Phase 2) carries symmetry
   breaking in the encoding: parent `G` is constrained by transposition
   lex-leader clauses on adjacent vertices in sorted-degree order, and any
   CEGAR blocking clause blocks the full isomorphism class found by nauty,
   not one labelling.  Exact enumeration of degree-preserving bijections is
   used only when the product of the factorials of the degree-class sizes is
   at most `10^5`; the threshold is fixed here, before any run.
3. **Regime statement for order 14.**  The exact-enumeration regime excludes
   twin-heavy graphs, which is where every known near-miss lives.  Order-14
   MaxSAT therefore explores the complement of the near-miss region.  That is
   stated as the purpose, not discovered after the fact.
4. **Provenance.**  Ivanov's family is arXiv:2608.11930 (12 August 2026)
   `[LIT-READ]` (HTML full text): 78-vertex pair with 51 common cards,
   families with overlap fraction tending to `1 - 1/r`.  McKay 2022
   `[LIT-READ]` (full text): no common-card maxima are computed anywhere in
   the paper; its Theorem 3.1 lists the verified classes reproduced in
   `LITERATURE_CHECK_2026-09-01.md`.  No exhaustive maximum common-card value
   at order 13 or 14 was found in any source; the only order-14 reference
   value is the construction bound `2 floor(13/3) = 8`.  Thatte and Oliveira
   arXiv:1301.4121 `[LIT-READ]` (full text): the only numerical examples are
   connected graphs on four vertices (full rank) and a directed-graph example
   of rank 4; no rank at order 5 or above.  Thatte EJC 2005 and JGT 2020
   `[LIT-ABSTRACT]`.  Kocay's edge-identity ratio computations, cited by
   Thatte and Oliveira `[LIT-READ]`, concern edge reconstruction.

## Phase 1c, resolved by proof: twin-class blow-ups

Ivanov's construction blows quotient vertices up into false-twin classes and
carries the difference in the class sizes.  The following settles the whole
mechanism.

**Setting.**  `Q` twin-free on `m` vertices, `Gamma = Aut(Q)`, `a` in
`Z_{>=1}^m`, `G(a)` the blow-up with independent classes of sizes `a_i`.
Since `Q` is twin-free and all classes are nonempty, the classes of `G(a)`
are its maximal false-twin classes, so `G(a) ~= G(b)` iff `b` is in
`Gamma a`.  Deleting from a class of size at least 2 gives `G(a - e_i)`;
deleting a singleton cell gives a blow-up of `Q - s`, which has at most
`m-1` twin classes and is never isomorphic to a blow-up of `Q`.

**Theorem (vector decks).**  `[PROVED]`  Let `Gamma <= Sym(m)` and let
`a, b` be integer vectors.  Suppose the multisets
`{{ Gamma(a - e_i) : a_i copies }}` and `{{ Gamma(b - e_j) : b_j copies }}`
coincide.  Let `mu` be the minimum entry of `a` over the coordinates that
are allowed to be decremented (all coordinates, or the non-singleton cells in
the graph setting), and suppose `mu >= 3` in the graph setting so that the
decremented value `mu - 1` exceeds every singleton entry.  Then `b` is in
`Gamma a`.

*Proof.*  Choose `i` with `a_i = mu`.  The card `a - e_i` has a unique
coordinate equal to `mu - 1`, namely `i`.  By hypothesis some `j` and
`gamma` satisfy `gamma(a - e_i) = b - e_j`, and `j` is a decrementable
coordinate of `b` because singleton deletions produce cards of a different
kind.  The vector `b - e_j` therefore has a unique coordinate equal to
`mu - 1`, at `gamma(i)`.  If `gamma(i) = j` then `gamma a = b`.  Otherwise
`b_{gamma(i)} = mu - 1`, so the decrementable minimum `mu'` of `b` is at
most `mu - 1`.  Running the same argument from `b` gives either `b` in
`Gamma a` or `mu <= mu' - 1 <= mu - 2`, a contradiction.  QED.

**Corollary.**  `[PROVED]`  If every non-singleton class of `G(a)` and of
`G(b)` has size at least 3, equal decks force `G(a) ~= G(b)`.  A blow-up
counterexample must therefore contain a class of size exactly 2 in each
parent, and the card obtained by deleting from it must be matched with the
class correspondence displaced onto a singleton cell: `b = gamma a + e_j -
e_{gamma(i)}` with `a_i = 2` and `a_j = 1` up to `Gamma`.  Ivanov's family
is exactly of this shape with the port classes consecutive; its smallest
port class is the coordinate `i` above and its selector vertices are the
singletons.

**Computation.**  `[COMPUTED]`  `scripts/vector_deck_reconstruction.py`
(all classes of size at least 2, every twin-free `Q`): no collision for
`m <= 6` with sizes in `[2,6]` and `m = 7` with sizes in `[2,4]`, consistent
with the theorem.  `scripts/blowup_deck_search.py` (singleton cells allowed,
decks of the actual graphs computed with nauty, parents compared by
certificate, hits replayed through the Python backends) is the live search;
results are appended below.

## Phase 1a: Kocay covering-number rank

`scripts/kocay_kernel.py` builds, for every `n`-vertex graph type `X`, the
covering numbers `c(F_1, F_2; X)` for sequences with `F_2 = star(v) u Ex`
(`|Ex| <= emax`) and `F_1` the complementary subgraph, plus the all-isolated
row, and computes the exact rank over `GF(2^31 - 1)`.  Controls: the
identity is checked by brute force on random triples at order 5, and every
kernel vector is inspected on disconnected columns (Kocay's theorem says the
complete system determines them).  Results appended below.

## Phase 1b: named-colour reconstruction census

`scripts/sigma_census.c` enumerates every local-map system at order `n`
with `sigma_0` normalised to the identity and `sigma_1` lexicographically
minimal under conjugation by `Sym({2..n-1})`, computes the generated edge
partition, and searches for an exact-label globalizer by identity check,
then signature-pruned backtracking.  A system with at least two classes and
no globalizer is a counterexample to reconstruction of edge-coloured
complete graphs with named colours, and every such counterexample arises
this way.  Order 5 reproduces the repository's earlier census (zero hits).
Results appended below.

**Blow-up search, quotient order 8** `[COMPUTED]`: 8,047 twin-free
quotients, cell sizes 1..2, 2,060,032 vectors, zero collisions.

**Two-degree graphs.**  `[PROVED]`  If the degree set of `G` is `{k, k'}`
with `|k - k'| >= 2`, then in any card `G - v` the neighbours of `v` are
exactly the vertices whose degree is outside `{k, k'}`, so `G` is
reconstructible from a single card.  The open two-degree case is therefore
consecutive degrees `{k, k+1}`, which is the MathOverflow question cited in
`GRC_AUDIT_2026-08-31.md`.  `[LIT-READ]` McKay 2022, Theorem 3.1(h) together
with complementation covers every consecutive pair at order 14 (`[0,5]`,
`[5,6]`, `[6,7]` and their complements `[7,8]`, `[8,13]`), so the first open
consecutive-degree instances are at order 15.

**Kocay families have a counting ceiling.**  `[PROVED]`  Rows indexed by
pairs `(F_1, F_2)` with `F_2` drawn from a bounded set of types number about
`psi(n-1)` times a constant, while the columns number `psi(n)`, and
`psi(n)/psi(n-1)` grows like `2^{n-1}/n`.  Hence for `n >= 10` no family
with bounded `F_2` can have full rank, and the full-rank results at orders
5 to 7 are partly a small-order effect.  Any proof of the conjecture through
Kocay identities must use second graphs of unbounded size.

clause "tau is not an isomorphism `G -> G xor Delta`" for the tau nauty
