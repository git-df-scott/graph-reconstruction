# Graph Reconstruction Conjecture — Internet Clue Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-08-31  
**Scope:** ordinary Kelly–Ulam conjecture: finite simple undirected graphs.  
**Campaign verdict:** no accepted counterexample found.

## Exact target

For a finite simple graph `G`, the deck is the multiset

```text
D(G) = {{ G - v : v in V(G) }}
```

where cards are considered up to graph isomorphism and multiplicity is retained.
A genuine counterexample requires explicit non-isomorphic finite simple undirected
graphs `G,H`, with `|V(G)| = |V(H)| >= 3`, satisfying `D(G) = D(H)` exactly as
multisets.

The following are not counterexamples by themselves: equal degree sequences,
cospectrality, equal characteristic polynomials, equal proper-subgraph counts,
equal card sets with different multiplicities, or counterexamples for digraphs,
hypergraphs, or infinite graphs.

## Executive findings

1. The ordinary conjecture remains open. No accepted finite simple undirected
   counterexample was located in the sources reviewed.
2. McKay's exhaustive computation verifies ordinary and set reconstruction through
   13 vertices. Any finite search must begin at `n >= 14`.
3. The standard reduction makes 2-connected graphs the central unresolved terrain.
4. Almost every graph is reconstructible from only three cards, so random search is
   strategically poor. A counterexample would be exceptional and structured.
5. Kelly's Lemma forces hypomorphic graphs to agree on every proper subgraph count.
6. The strongest classical clue is pseudo-similarity: vertices can have isomorphic
   deletion cards without being in the same automorphism orbit.
7. The important global obstruction is compatibility: independently chosen card
   isomorphisms need not assemble into one parent-graph isomorphism.
8. The 2024 vertex-substitution proof claim has a corrigendum and remains disputed;
   current specialist-facing sources still treat the full conjecture as open.

## Strongest research clues

### A. Target the 2-connected residue

Because disconnected graphs and many cut-vertex constructions are controlled, the
most defensible finite target class is 2-connected, nonregular, nonplanar or outside
known planar subclasses, and not a known product or interval-type graph.

### B. Target pseudo-similarity, not ordinary symmetry

Repeated cards caused by an actual automorphism are usually harmless: the symmetry
itself explains the repetition. The more promising configuration is

```text
G-u ~= G-v, but no automorphism of G maps u to v.
```

The candidate mechanism must then create two incompatible global systems of card
identifications while preserving every card with multiplicity.

### C. Test switching and module substitutions against full decks

Directed counterexamples and infinite examples suggest switching, orientation, or
end/module ambiguity. A finite undirected analogue would need a gadget operation that
preserves every vertex-deleted card, not merely spectra or a few local statistics.

### D. Use hypergraph and infinite examples only as mechanism sources

Incidence-graph encodings and finite “end ambiguity” gadgets are plausible clue
generators, but neither reduction is automatic. The encoding must be checked for
spurious distinguishing structure introduced by the gadgets.

### E. Card-minimal graphs are a clean laboratory

When all cards are distinct, multiplicity gives no easy information. Shuva reports
that the smallest card-minimal graphs occur at order six. These objects are useful
for studying whether unique card matching can still fail to globalize.

## Hard closure tests for any future candidate

For proposed `G,H`, require all of the following:

1. finite, simple, undirected, same order, at least three vertices;
2. exact canonical graph-isomorphism test proving `G` and `H` are non-isomorphic;
3. exact canonicalization of every card `G-v` and `H-w`;
4. equality of the canonical card multisets, including multiplicities;
5. independent replay using a second graph-isomorphism implementation;
6. exact edge count, degree sequence, connectivity, and subgraph-count controls;
7. a human-readable certificate: adjacency lists or graph6 strings plus card hashes;
8. no reliance on random sampling, floating point, spectra, or unlabeled-set equality.

## Present conclusion

The literature does not point toward random or small counterexamples. It points toward
an exceptional 2-connected graph pair whose decks are equal for a genuinely global
reason that defeats compatibility of card isomorphisms. The dissertation's most useful
lead is pseudo-similarity; the most important modern warning is that local card maps do
not automatically globalize.

**Status:** GRC counterexample not found; ordinary finite undirected conjecture remains open.
